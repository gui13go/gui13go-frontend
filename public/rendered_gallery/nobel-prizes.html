Albert Einstein

German-born Theoretical Physicist • Awarded in 1922 for 1921

The Breakthrough (In Plain English)

Discovery of the Law of the Photoelectric Effect: Proved that light propagates not merely as continuous waves, but as discrete quantized packets of energy (photons). When light strikes a metal surface, individual photons transfer their energy to electrons, knocking them free—which laid the experimental foundation for all quantum physics and modern solar cells.

Did You Know?

Einstein never won the Nobel Prize for his Theory of General Relativity! The conservative Nobel committee considered relativity too unproven and philosophically radical. In fact, due to committee deadlock, the 1921 Physics Prize was not awarded until 1922, specifically citing his 1905 paper on the photoelectric effect.

Why He Never Won the Medal

Because Hawking radiation from stellar black holes has a temperature of fractions of a nanokelvin ($10^{-8}$ K)—far below the cosmic microwave background—it has never been directly detected.
